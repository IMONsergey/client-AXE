# 09 — Reference deep dive / design extraction v2

This pass treats the supplied prototype strictly as information architecture. The visual system is rebuilt from the reference set.

## Core synthesis

ACE should feel like **an international market-infrastructure institution with a product-grade digital surface**.

The final system is not a visual average of the references. It combines two different roles:

- **Institutional shell:** WFE + CME Group + Gafta
- **Product core:** Stripe + Ramp + Paystand

Betterment and Acorns remain secondary references for pacing and explanatory clarity only.

---

## WFE — what to extract

### 1. Scale first
The WFE homepage establishes category authority immediately and places market-scale numbers directly near the top of the experience.

**ACE application:**
- `08 exchanges / 07 countries / ~15 considering membership` becomes a structural proof rail, not a small card;
- proof is visible before the user reaches the About section.

### 2. Editorial authority
WFE uses large headline language and allows institutional statements to occupy real visual space.

**ACE application:**
- hero headline can be materially larger than in the wireframe;
- secondary text should be short, sober and highly legible;
- avoid marketing-card clutter around the main statement.

### 3. Research / news / governance feel like core outputs
WFE treats research and regulatory material as evidence of institutional authority.

**ACE application:**
- declaration and documents should be designed as institutional proof objects;
- documents must look important, not like downloadable attachments.

### 4. International-system semantics
WFE repeatedly frames itself through markets, infrastructure and global flows rather than through generic association language.

**ACE application:**
- interface labels can use restrained market-infrastructure vocabulary: Network, Infrastructure, Framework, Membership, Documents, Trade Requests.

---

## CME Group — what to extract

### 1. Website feels operational
CME pages behave like interfaces to a live marketplace: products, market activity, data, tools and access live in one navigational system.

**ACE application:**
- introduce compact system rails, module labels and utility navigation;
- Trade Requests must feel like the operational side of ACE, not a marketing CTA.

### 2. Dense information with discipline
CME supports large information volumes without abandoning hierarchy.

**ACE application:**
- header may be dense but grid-aligned;
- use small uppercase metadata, rules, codes and index numbers as secondary architecture;
- density belongs in controlled rails, not random cards.

### 3. Dark market modules
CME can use dark/navy surfaces to signal market/data environments while keeping the wider experience institutional.

**ACE application:**
- one dark market-infrastructure chapter is appropriate;
- do not turn the whole site into a trading terminal.

### 4. Market activity strip
CME often gives immediate access to market/product activity through horizontal structures.

**ACE application:**
- use a horizontal ACE proof / network rail directly after or inside the hero;
- no fake prices, tickers or volume data.

---

## Gafta — what to extract

### 1. Formal service taxonomy
Contracts, arbitration, registers, policy and membership are treated as serious institutional products.

**ACE application:**
- three ACE directions should read as formal infrastructure capabilities, not marketing features;
- governance and founding documents should be given comparable visual authority.

### 2. Membership legitimacy
Gafta makes membership categories and international participation explicit and navigable.

**ACE application:**
- the seven countries should be treated as a membership/network register, not pills or flags;
- future member expansion belongs in proof language, not speculative map graphics.

### 3. Document systems
Gafta uses searchable, structured records rather than decorative download cards.

**ACE application:**
- design declaration as a registry row / record with type, title, context and action.

---

## Stripe — what to extract

### 1. Hero confidence
Stripe uses very large typography, short explanatory copy and immediate product context.

**ACE application:**
- hero must have a decisive message and generous whitespace;
- text hierarchy must do most of the visual work.

### 2. Infrastructure is shown, not only claimed
Stripe pairs brand statements with concrete product UI / integration diagrams.

**ACE application:**
- show a conceptual ACE system object next to the hero: network topology, rails, or structured platform window;
- the object should be clearly conceptual and must not invent market data.

### 3. Modules work individually and together
Stripe explains a large platform through related modules rather than a flat feature list.

**ACE application:**
- `Inter-exchange Links → Price Agency → Trading Platform` becomes one infrastructure stack;
- the visual connection between the three is as important as each module itself.

### 4. Layered surfaces
Stripe often uses quiet background shifts and nested product surfaces to create depth without heavy decoration.

**ACE application:**
- subtle cool-white / blue-white surfaces are allowed;
- avoid generic shadows, glassmorphism and rainbow gradients.

---

## Ramp — what to extract

### 1. Product proof adjacent to the claim
Ramp frequently pairs a large statement with a tangible product window or workflow fragment.

**ACE application:**
- Trade Requests can be represented by a sober conceptual access panel;
- do not invent screens that imply functionality the client has not confirmed.

### 2. Strong section headlines
Ramp gives each capability its own simple, decisive statement and large typographic scale.

**ACE application:**
- each infrastructure layer may have an oversized name and one short explanatory paragraph;
- avoid equal-weight 3-card SaaS layouts.

### 3. Modular workflow language
Ramp decomposes complex finance operations into a coherent platform.

**ACE application:**
- show the three strategic directions as a sequential operating model.

---

## Paystand — what to extract

### 1. Network narrative
Paystand's strongest transferable idea is that value comes from the connected network, not from a standalone feature.

**ACE application:**
- visual metaphor should be routes / nodes / shared rails / connected institutions;
- country names can appear as endpoints in a network register.

### 2. Portal as entry point into the network
Paystand frames its portal as an access layer to the network.

**ACE application:**
- Trade Requests should read as `Access the ACE operating layer`, not `another website section`.

### 3. Explain how the system works
Paystand repeatedly uses process sequencing.

**ACE application:**
- infrastructure chapter can communicate `connect → discover price → execute` as a three-stage flow.

---

## Betterment + Acorns — limited extraction

Use only for:
- calmer whitespace;
- approachable explanatory copy;
- reduction of cognitive load;
- clear, non-technical supporting language.

Do not use:
- lifestyle imagery;
- consumer-investing emotional tone;
- playful illustrations;
- app-store / retail-finance visual language.

# Design rules for the next concepts

## R1 — No generic SaaS card wall
Cards are permitted only when they behave as product proof, records or modules in a system.

## R2 — No fake market dashboard
Do not invent prices, volume, turnover, latency, status or transaction metrics.

## R3 — No decorative world map
International scale is expressed through a network topology / register / route system using only the seven supplied countries. Any topology is explicitly conceptual.

## R4 — Proof rail is mandatory
`08 / 07 / ~15` must appear as top-level evidence in every concept.

## R5 — Trade Requests is an operating layer
It receives strong placement in header + hero and, where useful, a conceptual product-access module.

## R6 — Documents are institutional proof
The declaration is rendered as a record, registry entry or formal proof object.

## R7 — Brand colours are signals
- Blue `#005EB8`: primary action / route / key highlight
- Red `#AF272F`: secondary institutional signal / document marker
- Green `#00B140`: tertiary network / connection signal
- Gray `#5B5B5B`: secondary text

No large three-colour decorative fills.

## R8 — Typography carries the brand
Intended production families remain Echoes Sans + Pragmatica. Until licensed files are supplied, fallbacks are only a temporary approximation.

## R9 — Exact prototype order is preserved conceptually
All design directions still contain:
1. Header
2. Hero
3. Scale proof
4. About / institutional origin
5. Three infrastructure directions
6. Countries / network participation
7. Founding declaration
8. Trade Requests access logic

## R10 — Different concepts must differ in architecture, not colour only
The five directions use different composition systems:
- authority grid;
- market network;
- editorial institution;
- product infrastructure;
- registry / ledger.
