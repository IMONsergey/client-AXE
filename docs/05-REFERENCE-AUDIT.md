# 05 — Reference audit

Research date: **2026-08-12**

This is the reference system for the ACE design concept. The supplied prototype is treated only as a content / IA wireframe. The visual language must be constructed from the references below rather than inherited from the prototype.

## Target synthesis

ACE should combine two different design archetypes:

1. **Institutional market infrastructure** — WFE, CME Group, Gafta.
   - strict grid;
   - structural clarity;
   - governance and documentation;
   - data as proof;
   - global scale;
   - restrained, credible visual tone.

2. **Modern financial product platform** — Stripe, Ramp, Paystand.
   - large confident typography;
   - platform-first framing;
   - modular product storytelling;
   - strong quantitative proof;
   - visible system / network logic;
   - a sense that the organisation operates infrastructure, not only describes itself.

Betterment and Acorns are secondary references for readability and progressive explanation only. They must not influence the institutional identity.

Recommended weighting:

- **20% WFE** — association authority + global market proof;
- **20% CME Group** — operating-market scale + data density;
- **10% Gafta** — membership, governance, documents and trade legitimacy;
- **20% Stripe** — infrastructure language + typography + modular system thinking;
- **15% Ramp** — decisive product hierarchy + quantified proof + platform decomposition;
- **10% Paystand** — network / rails narrative + connected-system visual logic;
- **5% Betterment / Acorns** — clarity, breathing room and progressive disclosure.

The result should feel like **a new international market-infrastructure institution with a real digital operating layer** — not an NGO brochure and not a fintech startup landing page.

## WFE — The World Federation of Exchanges

Official sources reviewed:

- https://www.world-exchanges.org/
- https://www.world-exchanges.org/about
- https://www.world-exchanges.org/our-work/statistics
- https://focus.world-exchanges.org/

### What matters

WFE establishes authority before persuasion. Its homepage frames the organisation at the scale of the market itself, then immediately supports that claim with large market-infrastructure statistics and later with research, statistics and institutional publications.

Current WFE communication prominently foregrounds:

- market infrastructure providers;
- listed companies;
- market capitalisation;
- statistics and research;
- regulatory / market topics;
- a global industry-association role.

### What ACE should borrow

- **Facts immediately adjacent to the main statement.** Scale is proof, not footer content.
- **Editorial confidence.** Large statements can carry the page without decorative hero imagery.
- **Institutional section architecture.** Clear labels, rules and typographic hierarchy rather than stacked SaaS cards.
- **Research / document credibility.** Publications and formal material look like first-class content.
- **Global-market language.** ACE should speak as infrastructure for markets, not as a club of organisations.

### What not to borrow

- A news-heavy homepage as the primary identity.
- Conservative association-site pacing if it makes ACE feel static.
- Dense editorial archives on the first conceptual screen.

### Direct implication for ACE

The `8 / 7 / ~15` facts belong at first-screen level. The Association’s declaration, documents and future data outputs should be visually treated as evidence objects, not downloads buried in a sidebar.

## CME Group

Official sources reviewed:

- https://www.cmegroup.com/
- https://www.cmegroup.com/markets.html
- https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data.html
- https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/browse-data.html

### What matters

CME does not behave like a corporate brochure. The site behaves like a public interface to a market environment: products, market activity, data, liquidity, research and tools coexist in one system.

The strongest patterns are:

- market activity is visible and current;
- quantitative information is integrated into the main experience;
- product / asset-class taxonomy is explicit;
- headings are direct and authoritative;
- complex information is organised through strict modular hierarchy.

### What ACE should borrow

- **Operating-system feeling.** The website should suggest that something is connected, measured and functioning behind it.
- **Data modules inside editorial layouts.** Numbers can sit directly next to narrative content without becoming dashboard clutter.
- **Strong category labelling.** Small uppercase / metadata labels can help separate institutional layer, infrastructure layer and platform layer.
- **Disciplined density.** ACE can feel substantial without becoming visually heavy.
- **Dark technical modules as contrast.** A dark platform / network area can work inside a mostly light institutional system.

### What not to borrow

- Trading-terminal density.
- Excessive product navigation before ACE has enough real content to justify it.
- Generic dark-blue finance aesthetics.

### Direct implication for ACE

The hero and Trade Requests should feel like entry points into a market ecosystem, while `About`, governance and documents remain clearly institutional.

## Gafta

Official sources reviewed:

- https://www.gafta.com/
- https://www.gafta.com/Membership
- https://www.gafta.com/arbitration

### What matters

Gafta is valuable less as a visual donor and more as a model of **institutional legitimacy**. Membership, contracts, arbitration, trade assurance, policy, training and formal documentation are concrete services of an international trade body.

Its current site strongly communicates:

- international membership;
- formal membership categories;
- standards / contracts;
- arbitration and dispute resolution;
- trade policy;
- governance / membership rules;
- downloadable institutional documents.

### What ACE should borrow

- **Governance is content.** Formal documents, rules, bodies and membership are not secondary legal clutter.
- **Membership scale as credibility.** Countries / exchanges should look like a network of institutions, not a tag cloud.
- **Formal taxonomy.** Clear separation between association, members, governance, documents and operating services.
- **Document UX.** Documents should expose type, status and context wherever source data allows it.

### What not to borrow

- Carousel-led homepage logic.
- Traditional association-card styling.
- Small corporate typography and generic brochure blocks.

### Direct implication for ACE

The declaration and future governance documents should have visual weight comparable to product modules. This is one of the main devices that prevents the site from looking like a speculative fintech startup.

## Stripe

Official source reviewed:

- https://stripe.com/

### What matters

Stripe’s current homepage is built around one extremely clear platform proposition, followed by modular capabilities and large proof metrics. It frames itself as financial infrastructure, then repeatedly demonstrates that infrastructure through product relationships, enterprise use cases and operating statistics.

The useful patterns are:

- one dominant value proposition;
- very large typography;
- modular capabilities designed to work individually or together;
- major proof metrics displayed as part of the brand narrative;
- product visuals as evidence rather than decoration;
- high whitespace and confident pacing.

### What ACE should borrow

- **Infrastructure-first headline logic.** The first screen should state what system ACE is building, not merely repeat the legal name.
- **One system, several layers.** Inter-exchange links, price agency and trading platform should read as parts of one infrastructure stack.
- **Large proof numbers.** `8 / 7 / ~15` should be treated with the same confidence Stripe gives its global operational metrics.
- **Progressive disclosure.** Complex functionality can start with a simple headline and reveal detail below.
- **Product visual as proof.** Trade Requests can appear through interface fragments / system states once we have truthful source material.

### What not to borrow

- Stripe gradients.
- Diagonal brand motifs.
- Soft 3D / decorative objects.
- Startup-style friendliness or developer-centric language that conflicts with the Association’s institutional role.

### Direct implication for ACE

Use Stripe’s hierarchy, spacing and systems thinking, not Stripe’s graphic signature.

## Ramp

Official sources reviewed:

- https://ramp.com/
- https://ramp.com/products
- https://ramp.com/about-us

### What matters

Ramp is exceptionally strong at turning a complex finance stack into a simple product story. Its current homepage leads with a short benefit statement, immediately reinforces scale / customer proof, and then decomposes the platform into distinct workflows under one umbrella.

Useful current patterns include:

- a short, memorable main statement;
- proof through customer / outcome metrics;
- one platform → several workflows;
- strong product labels with one-line outcome statements;
- sections that demonstrate what disconnected systems become when unified.

### What ACE should borrow

- **Headline compression.** Institutional language does not need to be bureaucratic.
- **Platform decomposition.** Three ACE directions can be presented as layers of one system, not three feature cards.
- **Outcome-led microcopy.** Each direction should explain what changes in the market infrastructure, not just name the service.
- **Numbers as evidence.** Facts should have typographic priority.
- **A visible relationship between modules.** The user should understand that the system compounds in value when components connect.

### What not to borrow

- Lead-generation forms in the hero.
- AI-agent language.
- Rounded SaaS card farms.
- Excessive logos / testimonials.
- High-conversion B2B SaaS tone.

### Direct implication for ACE

The block containing Inter-exchange Links / Price Agency / Trading Platform should become a connected platform diagram or structured three-layer system, not three equal white cards with coloured icons.

## Paystand

Official sources reviewed:

- https://www.paystand.com/
- https://www.paystand.com/payments
- https://www.paystand.com/payments/paystand-bank-network
- https://www.paystand.com/receivables/network-portal

### What matters

Paystand is useful because it repeatedly frames its product as a **network / rails / operating layer** between businesses rather than as a collection of disconnected features. Its story is chaptered around operating outcomes and explains how participants connect through one network.

### What ACE should borrow

- **Network as a core narrative.** ACE exists between exchanges / markets, so relationships are more important than isolated institution icons.
- **Rails / connection metaphor.** Cross-border links can be visualised as structured routes, nodes and states.
- **Chaptered product narrative.** Each major capability can have a short functional label and a larger human-readable statement.
- **System diagrams.** Show how participants, data and trading functions connect, provided the diagram remains truthful and conceptual.

### What not to borrow

- Blockchain visual clichés.
- Bright fintech gradients.
- Aggressive sales copy.
- Generic "network effect" claims that ACE cannot substantiate yet.

### Direct implication for ACE

The country / member area should become part of a network system rather than a row of pill tags.

# Cross-reference patterns to use

## 1. Grid before decoration

The base should come from WFE / CME / Gafta: strong content grid, clear alignment, rules, large whitespace and restrained surfaces.

## 2. One primary proposition

From Stripe / Ramp: the hero must communicate a single infrastructure-level idea in one glance.

Recommended semantic structure:

- organisation identity;
- one large infrastructure claim;
- short explanation;
- primary platform CTA;
- proof metrics;
- visible network / system object.

## 3. Proof is part of design

From WFE / CME / Stripe / Ramp: numbers are not supporting copy. They are structural anchors.

For the current concept, only these metrics are approved:

- `8` exchanges;
- `7` participating countries;
- `~15` countries considering membership.

Do not invent market volume, turnover, capitalisation, number of transactions or member-market share.

## 4. Modules must belong to one system

From Stripe / Ramp / Paystand:

- Inter-exchange Links = connectivity layer;
- Price Agency = price-discovery / data layer;
- Trading Platform = execution / transaction layer.

The visual composition should show these as connected layers of infrastructure.

## 5. Governance must remain visible

From WFE / Gafta:

- declaration;
- documents;
- governance bodies;
- membership;
- institutional history.

These elements prevent the modern product language from turning ACE into a startup aesthetic.

## 6. Global scale must be experienced, not merely stated

Do not reduce countries to rounded tags. Use a restrained network / route / region system, country list or structured matrix that expresses cross-border connectivity.

## 7. One dark technical surface is useful; a dark website is not

A predominantly light institutional canvas should be interrupted by one or two high-contrast infrastructure modules. This borrows CME’s seriousness and product-tech contrast without copying a trading terminal.

## 8. Corporate colours are signals

Brand guide values:

- Blue `#005EB8`
- Red `#AF272F`
- Green `#00B140`
- Cool Gray `#5B5B5B`

Use them as functional signals, thin rules, status accents and controlled highlights. Do not build large equal red / green / blue zones.

## 9. Typography should carry the visual weight

Brand guide:

- **Echoes Sans Regular / Bold** — headings and accents;
- **Pragmatica Book / Bold** — text.

The modernity should come primarily from scale, spacing and composition, not effects.

## 10. Avoid the two failure modes

### Failure mode A — "international association website"

Symptoms:

- grey hero card;
- small logo + long navigation;
- standard icon cards;
- tiny metrics;
- PDF buried in sidebar;
- country pills;
- no operating-system feeling.

### Failure mode B — "fintech startup landing"

Symptoms:

- gradients / blobs;
- excessive rounded cards;
- decorative dashboard mockups;
- startup voice;
- artificial KPIs;
- glowing network lines;
- motion for spectacle.

ACE must occupy the narrow middle: **institutional authority + product-system clarity**.
